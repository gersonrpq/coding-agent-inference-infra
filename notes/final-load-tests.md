## Closing the course: KV protection and a worker that comes back (decision [60](../ARCHITECTURE.md#decision-60); pre-registered before running)

Added because the course asks for them and the data did not show them: Part 5 "KV full after admit: shed or preempt?" and "after a worker
returns: slam it at 100 % or ramp while p99 holds?". Code first (unit-tested): `should_shed(req, snap)`, the engine queue depth as a tie-breaker of
`pick`, a `Shed` answer (`no_healthy_worker`) and never picking a worker that does not answer its metrics, and a ramp of a returning worker
(weight 0.25 -> 1 over 60 s, the clock stops while the fleet TTFT p99 is above 10 s).

| Id | Run | Question | Prediction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V7a KV full | `metrics/probes/kv_probe.py`, 12 requests of ~60K tokens at once, default threshold 0.95 | how full can the KV get after admission, and does the engine preempt? | by construction 6 sequences x 64K cannot take more than 88 % of a 435K-token pool: the worst worker peaks at 80-88 %, **zero retractions**, no `kv_pressure` shed; the 12 requests are all served (slowly: TTFT of the queue behind 12 long prefills) |
| V7b the shed path | same probe with `KV_SHED_THRESHOLD=0.70` on the deployment | does `kv_pressure` act, and does it spare cached prefixes? | the request with a NEW prefix gets a 503 `kv_pressure` in under 1 s; the request that reuses the system prompt of one of the 12 is admitted (200) |
| V8 ramp | `metrics/probes/ramp_probe.py`: 6 requests in flight, every one a new session; worker 1 restarted at 40 s | no traffic to a dead worker, and a gradual return | share of worker 1 about 0.5 before the restart (+-0.1); after the poller notices (<= 15 s) nothing is sent to it; failed requests are limited to the first window after the kill (<= 15); `orch_worker_available{w1}` goes 1 -> 0 -> 1; when it answers again `orch_worker_ramp_factor{w1}` starts at 0.25 and reaches 1 within 60-90 s, and its share in the first 15 s window after the return is <= 0.30 and >= 0.45 within 90 s |

Against the predictions:
- **F1 knee:** served per minute is flat (47.0, 56.8, 46.4: **held**, 56.8 a little above the 45-55 predicted), refusals 3.7 % at N = 16 and 38 % at N = 20 (**held**), but 70 % at N = 24 (predicted 55-65 %: **missed**) and the TTFT p99 is 13.7 s at N = 24 (predicted <= 10 s: **missed**; N = 16 and 20 held). The SLO (p99 <= 20 s) holds at all three loads; the knee for "refusals below 5 %" is N = 16.
- **F2 replicate (same seed 400):** `affload` 50.4 served/min against `least-busy` 43.6 (**+16 %**), p99 10.3 s against 11.5 s, refusals 48.8 % against 53.0 %, cached share 88 % against 74 %. So the extra refusals of `affload` seen in `er-n20` (42 % against 30 %) are **not reproduced**: with a paired seed `affload` refuses less. The two `affload` runs at N = 20 differ by 11 % in served per minute (56.8 with seed 120, 50.4 with seed 400; the seeds are different conversations): the prediction "within +-10 %" **missed by one point**, and 11 % is the noise level to keep in mind when reading differences between single runs.
- **F3 batch (20 % of the sessions):** interactive p99 7.4 s (**held**, within +20 % of 7.7 s), interactive refusals 1.4 %, no abandoned interactive call. Batch was **98 % refused** (373 of 427 attempts by `batch_pressure`, 46 by the cap): predicted "batch absorbs the refusals" held, but far harder than intended: the `batch_pressure` rule (fleet TTFT p99 > 4x p50) is true almost all the time in this workload, where p99 is naturally 7-8x p50 (p50 about 1 s, p99 about 7 s), so batch is sacrificed even though the interactive p99 is far below the 20 s SLO. Consequence: the rule needs a floor (see the open point in ARCHITECTURE decision [57](../ARCHITECTURE.md#decision-57)).
- **F4 regime B (120 s idle, N = 48):** the cache prediction **held strongly**: at the start of a turn only **27.5 %** of the prompt is read from cache (88.7 % in regime A, `fin-soak`), 70.8 % inside a turn; TTFT at the start of a turn p50 5.3 s. p99 14.9 s (<= 20 s: held). But 84.7 % of the attempts were refused (all by the cap): N = 48 open sessions is beyond what the system holds with these pauses; the capacity for open sessions is lower than the 46 estimated in the sweep protocol, and was not located (a point at N = 32 is part of the validation).
- **F5 soak (900 s, N = 16):** stable: 48.6 served/min, p99 7.1 s, refusals 1.7 %; served per minute by five-minute window 43.6 (it includes the warm-up minute), 48.2 and 49.4; KV used peaked at 57 % (never near the 95 % of `kv_pressure`), the engine queue never exceeded 2 per worker (K), no retraction, and the in-flight count returned to 0 at the end (no leaked place). The verdict of the script says "fail" only because the refusal rate (1.7 %) is above the 1 % it was set to.
- **What was not shown:** `kv_pressure` never fired (KV <= 62 % in every run) and the engine never retracted: the KV protection has no live evidence; `batch_pressure` did fire (376 times).

**2026-10-02 - V4b result and the mistake it exposed.** `fin3-batch` (floor on `batch_pressure` + a batch share counting only batch requests): batch served 40 calls (8.0/min, 3.6x V4: predicted 5x, **missed**), `batch_pressure` refusals 159 (-60 % against 393: predicted -90 %, **missed**), interactive p99 7.1 s (held), but **interactive refusals 39.5 %** (133 of 337; predicted <= 5 %, **missed**) and `batch_share` never fired (0). Reading: (1) the share I wrote counted the batch requests in flight, and 4 batch sessions can never have 10 in flight, so it could not act; the rule of the old counter was different and correct: batch enters only while the TOTAL in flight is below its limit, which keeps the last places for interactive calls. (2) The earlier reading of V4 ("the reservation mattered: 1.4 % -> 16.5 %") is not supported: in F3 batch was almost fully starved by the eager `batch_pressure` rule (8 served), so the interactive sessions saw an effective N of 16; as soon as batch is let in (V4: 11 served, V4b: 40 served) interactive refusals rise (16.5 %, 39.5 %) because the same 14 places serve more work. The refusal rate follows how much batch work is admitted. V4c tests the corrected rule.

**2026-10-02 - V4c result** (`fin4-batch`, batch admitted only while fewer than 10 requests of any class are in flight): interactive refusals **6.8 %** (16 of 234; predicted <= 10 %: held; V4b 39.5 %, V4 16.5 %), interactive p99 **9.7 s** (<= 10 s: held, barely), batch served 14 calls (2.8/min: between V4's 2.2 and V4b's 8.0, held). **Missed:** `batch_share` was not the main refusal reason of batch (150), `batch_pressure` still was (217; queue_full 27): the floor lowered it but did not remove it, because the fleet TTFT p99 the rule reads includes the batch calls themselves and sits near 10 s at N = 20. Batch remains almost fully refused (96.6 %) at this load, by design: at N = 20 the fleet is past its knee and interactive calls come first. The four batch runs side by side (interactive refusals / batch served): F3 old counter 1.4 % / 8, V4 native cap alone 16.5 % / 11, V4b floor + wrong share 39.5 % / 40, **V4c 6.8 % / 14**. The final rule is V4c's.

**2026-10-03 - V7a and V8 results; V7b needed a second try.**
- **V7a (KV full, default threshold): held.** 12 requests of about 60K tokens at once: the worst worker peaked at **83.0 %** of its pool (predicted 80-88 %), **0 retractions**, no `kv_pressure` shed, all 12 served (slowest 51 s: twelve long prefills queued). The 88 % ceiling is structural: 6 running sequences x 64K tokens cannot exceed it. - **V7b first try (threshold 0.70): the shed path did not fire, and the reason is a finding.** The probe sent the new-prefix request when its own 1-second sampling of the engine showed 71-75 % KV, and the request was admitted (200 after 17 s). Deviation from the pre-registered probe: the second try holds the KV full for about 50 s (1,500-token decodes) and sends the two requests 20 s after the KV passed 72 %, so the snapshot has caught up.

## Confirmation on the final code (V9; pre-registered 2026-10-03, before running)

F1-F5 ran with the gateway's own counter and the 20 s first-token cut. Since then the cap became LiteLLM's middleware, the cut was removed, and `place.py` gained the queue-depth tie-break, the ramp and `Shed`. V3-V5 re-checked N = 20, the batch rules and real agents; the knee at N = 24 and the soak were not repeated. They are the two headline claims (overload behaviour, stability), so they are rerun once on the rebuilt cluster with the **same seeds** as the originals (`SEED=100` for N = 24, `SEED=700` for the soak) and the same duration and warm-up.

Decision rule: if both hold, the headline numbers of DESIGN.md and README stay as they are and V9 is cited as the confirmation on the final code. If one fails, the numbers are replaced by the new ones in the documents and the plots, and the difference is explained in `notes/findings.md`.
